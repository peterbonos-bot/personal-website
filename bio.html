Peter Bonos is a musician, bandleader, audio engineer and event producer based in Piedmont, California. A native of the San Francisco Bay Area, he plays trumpet, oud and euphonium in klezmer, Balkan brass, Turkish and related Eastern European and Mediterranean traditions.

Peter leads Orchestra Euphonos, an Eastern European folk and klezmer ensemble whose Romanian and Moldovan repertoire has grown through mentorship and collaboration with accordionist Sergiu Popa and other guest artists. He is a founding member of Fanfare Zambaleta and co-founder of Duygu & Friends, and has performed with Alaturca Connection, Saul Goodman’s Klezmer Brass Band, Zirzuví and other Bay Area ensembles.

At Hampshire College, Peter studied with jazz multi-instrumentalist Marty Ehrlich and explored improvisation, composition and electroacoustic music. His interest in klezmer developed through studies with Brian Bender, Adrianne Greenbaum and Hankus Netsky, followed by further study at KlezKanada. Travels in the Balkans and Turkey have continued to inform his musical work.

Since 2020, Peter has produced the Berkeley Balkan Bacchanal at The Starry Plough. His production and community arts work also includes the Jewish Music Festival, Voice of Roma’s Herdeljezi festival, the SF Sephardic Music Festival and the EEFC Balkan Music Workshop. He previously served as Program Manager at Congregation Sherith Israel and worked at the JCC East Bay. Alongside performing and producing events, he offers private trumpet lessons and live audio support.

Peter Bonos is a musician, bandleader, audio engineer, educator and event producer based in Piedmont, California. A native of the San Francisco Bay Area, he plays trumpet, oud and euphonium, working across klezmer, Balkan brass, Turkish and Anatolian music, and related Eastern European and Mediterranean traditions. His career connects performance, musical exploration and community arts production.

Peter began studying trumpet as an adolescent in Santa Rosa, receiving private instruction from David Fichera and Pete Estabrook. At Santa Rosa High School, he performed with Chamber Winds and Symphonic Band. He also composed and played electric guitar with his first band, the Set-Up, and performed trumpet with local funk and rock musicians and the new music chamber ensemble Triste Sin Richard, formed by cellist Chris Votek.

At Hampshire College, Peter was mentored by jazz saxophonist and multi-instrumentalist Marty Ehrlich. His studies ranged from modern jazz and improvisation to electroacoustic music and sound design. He developed experimental approaches to a resonating steel plate using Max/MSP, later concentrating on acoustic solo trumpet and small ensemble performance. He also performed with Hampshire’s Jazz Improvisation Orchestra and the Smith College Gamelan Ensemble, and participated in a community performance of Terry Riley’s In C in Brattleboro, Vermont.

Peter discovered a passion for Eastern European music while studying klezmer with trombonist Brian Bender and flutist Adrianne Greenbaum. He went on to form a student ensemble under the mentorship of Hankus Netsky and founded the Frelekh Valley Orkestar, which performed at the Yiddish Book Center. He continued his study of klezmer and Yiddish music on scholarship at KlezKanada in Quebec, working with artists including Frank London and Susan Hoffman Watts. He has also served on the board of KlezCalifornia and performed at its Yiddish Culture Festival.

Study abroad in Berlin in 2007 brought Peter into contact with community brass bands and experimental musicians. Later travels took him through Eastern Europe and Turkey. In 2017, he studied at a Roma music camp in Skopje with Sani Rifati and saxophonist and clarinetist Ferus Mustafov, and visited the Guča brass festival in Serbia. In Turkey in 2022, he studied with Salih Korkut Peker and Christina Gkika at Music Köyu in Fethiye. During a return visit to Berlin in 2017, he performed with Big Beats Big Times in the inaugural Spiecher concert series, staged in a historic underground water cistern.

After relocating to the East Bay in 2010, Peter taught himself euphonium and became an auxiliary member of Inspector Gadje. He subsequently focused on Balkan trumpet playing with Fanfare Zambaleta, of which he is a founding member. The band has studied and performed with Nizo Alimov and Demiran Ćerimović, appeared at festivals around the Pacific Northwest, and performed regularly at Ashkenaz and Kafana Balkan. Fanfare Zambaleta was featured in the documentary Balkancisco and performed at its San Francisco premiere in 2024.

Peter formed Orchestra Euphonos in 2011. The ensemble’s klezmer and Eastern European folk repertoire developed through sustained mentorship and collaboration with Moldovan Roma accordionist Sergiu Popa and Romanian and Moldovan artists including Ionela Guzic, Marian Milița and Viorica Onuț. Performance highlights include the Oakland Athletics’ Jewish Heritage Night, Balkan Night Northwest and Balkan Fest San Diego. Peter also co-founded Duygu & Friends, performing Turkish, Anatolian and Mediterranean repertoire on oud and trumpet.

His collaborations span traditional music, experimental performance and recording. He has worked with Alaturca Connection, Saul Goodman’s Klezmer Brass Band, Zirzuví, Kali Orkestar, Ali Yagiz Sen, Shireen Nabatian, Dror Sinai and the Feldthouse Family Band. From 2011 to 2015, he performed trumpet and newly invented instruments with David Samas. Earlier collaborations include Tyler Green of the Lumerians and Jonas Golland of the Tiger Lillies. His recording work has included Tipsy, Fat Worm of Error, Two Prong, Justine Lucas and Jonah Uttal (Madame X). He has also performed with Bob Marsh, Jack Callahan, Michelle Alany and the Mystics, Taluna, Zebu, Jeremy Starpoli and Neil A. Young, known as Cloaca and Bromp Treb.

Alongside performing, Peter has built a career in concert production and community programming. At the Jewish Music Festival, he worked as Assistant Director and Technical Coordinator, helping produce concerts with artists including Yemen Blues, the Klezmatics, David Krakauer, Jewlia Eisenberg, Daniel Kahn and the Painted Bird, Diwan Saz and the Bustan Quartet. In 2011, he helped establish and manage the Ger Mandolin Ensemble, a musical revival project led by Mike Marshall, Eric Stein and Jeff Warshavsky and commissioned by Avner Yonai.

Since 2020, Peter has produced the Berkeley Balkan Bacchanal at The Starry Plough in Berkeley. His work also includes Voice of Roma’s Herdeljezi festival, the SF Sephardic Music Festival, and many years as Kafana manager and Dance Hall stage manager at the EEFC Balkan Music Workshop. He previously worked at the JCC East Bay and served as Program Manager at Congregation Sherith Israel in San Francisco from 2017 to 2026. Today, he continues to perform, produce community music events, teach trumpet and provide live audio and event support.
