Instruments

Trumpet and oud are my main instruments. I also play euphonium, bouzouki and guitar, moving between brass and string traditions across klezmer, Balkan, Turkish, Eastern European and Mediterranean music.

Five musical voices

Each instrument opens a different part of the repertoire—from the projecting, vocal quality of trumpet to the intimacy of oud and guitar, the warm low brass of euphonium, and the bright rhythmic character of bouzouki.

TrumpetMy primary brass instrument—used across klezmer, Balkan, Eastern European and ensemble work for melody, improvisation and a strong singing tone.
OudMy primary string instrument, especially central to Turkish, Anatolian and Mediterranean repertoire with Duygu & Friends and related collaborations.
EuphoniumA warm, resonant low-brass voice that I use in Balkan and Eastern European settings, adding depth beneath the brighter brass and reeds.
GuitarA supporting string instrument for accompaniment, songwriting and informal ensemble playing; this photo shows a 12-string acoustic guitar from my collection.
BouzoukiA long-necked plucked string instrument I use in Greek, Balkan and related Mediterranean repertoire.
Why these instruments

I like moving between brass and strings because each asks for a different kind of listening. Trumpet can lead a room; oud can draw it inward. Euphonium, bouzouki and guitar let me shift roles—from melody to rhythm to support—depending on the ensemble and tradition.
